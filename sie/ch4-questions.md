## Section 4.1 — Registration & Qualification (6 questions)

### Q1 (Scenario)

A college graduate passes the SIE exam in January 2026 but decides to work in management consulting before entering the securities industry. She finally secures sponsorship from a FINRA member firm in March 2030 and wants to sit for the Series 7. What is her SIE status?

**A.** Valid — the SIE remains active for 6 years from the date of passing  
**B.** Expired — the SIE is only valid for 4 years and lapsed in January 2030  
**C.** Valid — once passed, the SIE never expires regardless of employment status  
**D.** Expired — the SIE expires after 2 years if the candidate is not sponsored by a firm  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The SIE exam results are valid for exactly **4 years**. Since she passed in January 2026, the credential expired in January 2030. By March 2030, it has already lapsed and she must retake the SIE before sitting for the Series 7. **(A)** is wrong because the window is 4 years, not 6. **(C)** is wrong because the SIE absolutely expires. **(D)** is wrong because the 2-year window applies to top-off exams after leaving the industry, not to the SIE itself.

### Q2 (Scenario)

A registered representative leaves their firm to take a year-long sabbatical. When they return to the industry 18 months later, which of the following is true regarding their licensing?

**A.** They must retake both the SIE and their top-off exam because both have expired  
**B.** Their top-off exam is still valid because it remains active for 4 years after departure  
**C.** Their top-off exam is still valid because it remains active for 2 years after departure  
**D.** They must retake the SIE but not the top-off exam  

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Once fully registered, if a representative leaves the industry, their **top-off exams remain valid for exactly 2 years**. Since only 18 months have passed, the top-off exam is still valid. **(A)** is wrong because 18 months is within the 2-year window. **(B)** is wrong because the top-off validity window is 2 years, not 4 (the 4-year window applies to the SIE). **(D)** reverses the likely expiration order — the SIE has a longer 4-year validity window and would also still be valid.

### Q3 (Scenario)

A back-office employee at a broker-dealer processes customer checks and handles physical stock certificates daily. Her manager asks whether she needs to be fingerprinted under SEC Rule 17f-2. What is the correct answer?

**A.** No — fingerprinting is only required for registered representatives  
**B.** No — fingerprinting is only required for persons who interact directly with customers  
**C.** Yes — anyone who handles cash or securities, or supervises someone who does, must be fingerprinted  
**D.** Yes — but only if the employee has been with the firm for more than 90 days  

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Under **SEC Rule 17f-2**, any person who handles cash or securities, handles the original books and records related to cash/securities, or supervises anyone who does, must be fingerprinted. The fingerprints are submitted to the FBI for a criminal background check. **(A)** is wrong because the requirement extends beyond registered persons. **(B)** is wrong because customer-facing interaction is not the test — handling cash or securities is. **(D)** is fabricated — there is no 90-day waiting period.

### Q4 (Conceptual)

Which of the following events would trigger statutory disqualification from the securities industry?

**A.** A misdemeanor conviction for reckless driving  
**B.** A felony conviction for tax evasion 8 years ago  
**C.** An unpaid credit card balance resulting in collections  
**D.** A civil lawsuit filed by a former employer over a non-compete agreement  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** **Any felony conviction** within the past 10 years triggers statutory disqualification. Tax evasion 8 years ago falls within the 10-year lookback period. **(A)** is wrong because non-financial misdemeanors like reckless driving do not trigger disqualification — only misdemeanors involving money or securities (e.g., embezzlement, fraud, theft). **(C)** is wrong because unpaid consumer debts do not constitute statutory disqualification (though they may need to be disclosed). **(D)** is wrong because civil contract disputes do not trigger disqualification.

### Q5 (Scenario)

A registered representative based in Texas wants to start soliciting clients in California and New York. What additional regulatory step must the representative take before contacting prospects in those states?

**A.** File an amended Form U5 with FINRA listing the new states  
**B.** Register in California and New York under the applicable state Blue Sky Laws  
**C.** Obtain a separate FINRA membership for each state  
**D.** Pass the Series 66 exam, which automatically grants multi-state registration  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Under **Blue Sky Laws**, representatives must register in every individual state where they conduct business, typically by passing the Series 63 (or Series 66) and paying state registration fees. A rep in Texas soliciting clients in California and New York must be registered in all three states. **(A)** is wrong because Form U5 is for termination, not multi-state registration. **(C)** is wrong because FINRA membership is firm-level, not state-by-state for individuals. **(D)** is wrong because passing the Series 66 alone does not automatically register you — you must still apply and pay fees in each state.

### Q6 (Conceptual)

An individual has been expelled from a foreign securities regulator for fraudulent trading practices. Can this individual register with a FINRA member firm in the United States?

**A.** Yes — foreign regulatory actions have no bearing on U.S. registration  
**B.** Yes — but only if the individual waits 5 years after the expulsion  
**C.** No — being expelled or barred by a foreign equivalent regulator is a trigger for statutory disqualification  
**D.** No — but the individual may re-apply after paying a fine to FINRA  

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** **Statutory disqualification** is triggered by being expelled, barred, or currently suspended by an SRO (like FINRA) **or a foreign equivalent regulator**. The individual would need the firm to initiate an Eligibility Proceeding with FINRA to seek a waiver. **(A)** is wrong because foreign regulatory actions absolutely apply. **(B)** is wrong because there is no automatic 5-year waiting period. **(D)** is wrong because paying a fine does not override statutory disqualification.

## Section 4.2 — Continuing Education (4 questions)

### Q7 (Scenario)

A registered representative completes her Firm Element training by November but neglects to complete the Regulatory Element by December 31st. What is the immediate consequence?

**A.** She receives a formal written warning but may continue working  
**B.** She is fined $1,000 per day until the Regulatory Element is completed  
**C.** Her registration becomes CE Inactive — she cannot perform registered activities or receive commissions  
**D.** Her registration is immediately and permanently terminated by FINRA  

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** If a registered person fails to complete the Regulatory Element by December 31st, their registration automatically becomes **CE Inactive**. While CE Inactive, the individual cannot perform any duties requiring registration and cannot receive any compensation based on securities transactions. **(A)** is wrong because a warning is not the regulatory consequence — actual suspension of activity is. **(B)** is fabricated. **(D)** is wrong because termination only occurs if the person remains CE Inactive for a continuous **2-year** period, not immediately.

---

### Q8 (Scenario)

A registered representative has been CE Inactive for 20 consecutive months and has made no effort to complete the Regulatory Element. What will happen if they remain inactive for 4 more months?

**A.** FINRA will automatically reduce their CE Inactive status to a warning  
**B.** FINRA will administratively terminate their registration, requiring them to retake their qualifying exams  
**C.** The firm will be required to file a Form U5 but the representative may resume activity at any time  
**D.** The representative will be grandfathered back to active status under FINRA's safe harbor provision  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** If a registered person remains CE Inactive for a continuous **2-year period** (24 months), FINRA will administratively terminate their registration. To re-enter the industry, they would need to retake their qualifying exams from scratch. 20 months + 4 months = 24 months, triggering automatic termination. **(A)** is fabricated. **(C)** is wrong because termination is not a technicality — the person cannot simply resume. **(D)** is a fabricated provision.

### Q9 (Conceptual)

The Firm Element of Continuing Education differs from the Regulatory Element in several ways. Which of the following is an accurate distinction?

**A.** The Firm Element is administered by FINRA; the Regulatory Element is administered by each firm  
**B.** The Firm Element focuses on firm-specific products and services; the Regulatory Element covers industry-wide rules and ethics  
**C.** The Firm Element is required every 3 years; the Regulatory Element is required annually  
**D.** The Firm Element applies to all firm employees; the Regulatory Element applies only to registered representatives  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The **Firm Element** is created and delivered internally by the broker-dealer, tailored to the firm's own products, services, and business model. The **Regulatory Element** is standardized content from FINRA covering rules, regulations, and ethics. **(A)** reverses the two — FINRA administers the Regulatory Element, and the firm administers the Firm Element. **(C)** is wrong because both are now annual requirements. **(D)** is wrong because the Firm Element applies to "covered persons" (those who deal with customers and their supervisors), not all employees.

### Q10 (Scenario)

A broker-dealer's compliance department is preparing its annual Firm Element training plan. As part of this process, what must the firm conduct before designing the training content?

**A.** A Regulatory Element pretest to benchmark each representative's current knowledge  
**B.** A Needs Analysis that considers the firm's business model, recent complaints, new products, and regulatory changes  
**C.** An independent audit by FINRA to approve the proposed training curriculum  
**D.** A shareholder vote to allocate budget for the training program  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Before creating Firm Element training, the firm must conduct an annual **Needs Analysis** that accounts for the firm's business model, customer complaints from the previous year, changes in product offerings, and new regulatory priorities. The firm then creates a written training plan based on this analysis. **(A)** confuses the Firm Element with the Regulatory Element. **(C)** is wrong because FINRA does not pre-approve Firm Element content. **(D)** is fabricated.

## Section 4.3 — Form U4, Form U5 & Conduct (5 questions)

### Q11 (Scenario)

A registered representative is terminated from her firm on March 15th. By what date must the firm file Form U5 with FINRA, and what must the firm provide to the terminated representative?

**A.** Within 10 business days; the firm must provide a copy of the U5 only if the representative requests it in writing  
**B.** Within 30 days (by April 14th); the firm must provide a copy of the U5 to the terminated representative  
**C.** Within 60 days (by May 14th); the firm is not required to share the U5 with the employee  
**D.** Within 90 days; the firm must provide a summary but not the actual form  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** **Form U5** must be filed within **30 days** of a registered person leaving the firm, regardless of the reason for departure (resignation, termination, or layoff). The firm is also legally obligated to provide a copy of the U5 to the terminated employee within that same 30-day window. **(A)** is wrong on both the timeframe (it's 30 calendar days, not 10 business days) and the condition (no written request is needed). **(C)** and **(D)** use incorrect timeframes.

### Q12 (Scenario)

A registered representative is convicted of a misdemeanor involving securities fraud on June 1st. How quickly must the representative's Form U4 be updated to reflect this event?

**A.** Within 30 days, as with any routine change  
**B.** Within 10 days, because the event involves a statutory disqualification trigger  
**C.** Within 5 business days, under the SEC's expedited disclosure rule  
**D.** At the next annual U4 review, which occurs on the representative's registration anniversary  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Form U4 updates follow a two-tier timeline: routine changes (like a new home address) must be updated within **30 days**, but any update involving a **statutory disqualification event** — such as a misdemeanor conviction involving money or securities — must be filed within **10 days**. **(A)** is wrong because this is not a routine change. **(C)** is fabricated. **(D)** is wrong because there is no annual review schedule — updates are event-driven.

### Q13 (Scenario)

A registered representative quits her firm to "escape" a pending FINRA investigation into unauthorized trading in her client accounts. Her Form U5 is filed, and 18 months pass. Can FINRA still pursue disciplinary action against her?

**A.** No — once a representative leaves the industry, FINRA's jurisdiction ends immediately  
**B.** No — FINRA's jurisdiction ends 12 months after the U5 is filed  
**C.** Yes — FINRA retains jurisdiction over the individual for 2 years after the U5 filing  
**D.** Yes — FINRA retains permanent, lifetime jurisdiction over all former registered persons  

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** FINRA retains regulatory jurisdiction over an individual for **2 years** after their Form U5 is filed. Since only 18 months have passed, FINRA can still investigate and pursue disciplinary action, including barring the individual from the industry. **(A)** is wrong because jurisdiction does not end upon departure. **(B)** uses an incorrect timeframe. **(D)** is wrong because FINRA's post-departure jurisdiction is limited to 2 years, not lifetime.

### Q14 (Conceptual)

When a registered representative signs Form U4, they agree to a mandatory arbitration clause. What does this clause require?

**A.** All customer disputes must be settled through mediation before going to court  
**B.** Employment disputes with the firm must be resolved in binding arbitration, with certain exceptions for discrimination and sexual harassment claims  
**C.** The representative waives all rights to file regulatory complaints with FINRA  
**D.** The representative agrees to pay a mandatory annual arbitration insurance premium  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** By signing the U4, a registered person agrees to settle **employment disputes** with their firm through **binding arbitration** rather than through the court system. The only exceptions are claims involving statutory employment discrimination or sexual harassment, which may still be taken to court. **(A)** is wrong because the clause mandates arbitration, not mediation, and covers employer disputes. **(C)** is wrong because the arbitration clause does not waive the right to file regulatory complaints. **(D)** is fabricated.

### Q15 (Scenario)

A customer sends a detailed email to their broker complaining about unsuitable investment recommendations. The complaint does not involve theft, forgery, or misappropriation. How should the firm handle this complaint from a regulatory reporting standpoint?

**A.** Report the complaint to FINRA immediately (within 24 hours)  
**B.** Include the complaint in the firm's quarterly summary report to FINRA and retain the email on file for 4 years  
**C.** Report the complaint to the SEC within 10 business days  
**D.** No reporting is required because the complaint was submitted via email rather than a formal letter  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** A written complaint (which includes emails) that does not involve theft, forgery, or misappropriation must be included in the firm's **quarterly summary report** to FINRA (due by the 15th of the month following the quarter). The complaint must also be retained on file for **4 years** at the firm's Office of Supervisory Jurisdiction (OSJ). **(A)** is wrong because immediate reporting is only required for complaints involving theft, forgery, or misappropriation. **(C)** is fabricated. **(D)** is wrong because emails absolutely count as written complaints.

## Section 4.4 — Reportable Events & Conflicts of Interest (5 questions)

### Q16 (Scenario)

A registered representative works at a broker-dealer full-time but wants to start driving for a ride-sharing company on weekends to earn extra income. What must the representative do before starting this activity?

**A.** Nothing — ride-sharing is not a securities-related activity and requires no disclosure  
**B.** Provide prior written notice to their employing firm describing the outside business activity  
**C.** Obtain prior written approval from FINRA before engaging in any non-securities employment  
**D.** File an amended Form U5 listing the secondary employer  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Any employment or business activity outside the scope of a representative's relationship with their firm is an **Outside Business Activity (OBA)**, even if it has nothing to do with securities. OBAs require **prior written notice** to the employing firm. The firm then decides whether the activity creates a conflict of interest. **(A)** is wrong because all outside employment, including non-securities work like ride-sharing, requires notice. **(C)** is wrong because notice goes to the firm, not FINRA. **(D)** is wrong because Form U5 is for termination, not OBA reporting.

### Q17 (Scenario)

A registered representative helps a personal friend invest $50,000 in the friend's brother's unlisted startup company. The representative receives a $2,500 referral fee for facilitating the investment. This transaction was not disclosed to or processed through the representative's firm. What violation has occurred?

**A.** An Outside Business Activity (OBA) violation — the rep should have provided written notice  
**B.** A Private Securities Transaction (selling away) violation — the rep needed prior written approval from the firm because compensation was received  
**C.** No violation — private transactions between personal friends are exempt from FINRA rules  
**D.** A churning violation — the rep generated excessive commissions from the transaction  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** This is a **Private Securities Transaction (PST)**, commonly called "selling away." Because the representative received compensation ($2,500), they were required to obtain **prior written approval** from their firm before facilitating the transaction. The firm would then be required to record it on its books and supervise the activity. **(A)** is wrong because OBAs involve non-securities activities — this involved actual securities. **(C)** is wrong because there is no "friends and family" exemption from PST rules. **(D)** is wrong because churning involves excessive trading in a customer's own brokerage account, not facilitating outside investments.

### Q18 (Scenario)

A Municipal Finance Professional (MFP) who lives and votes in Chicago contributes $500 to the mayoral campaign of a candidate in Dallas, Texas. What consequence does this trigger under MSRB Rule G-37?

**A.** No consequence — $500 is within the permissible contribution limit  
**B.** The MFP receives a personal fine of $500 from the MSRB  
**C.** The firm is banned from engaging in negotiated municipal underwriting with the city of Dallas for 2 years  
**D.** The firm must return all municipal underwriting fees earned from Dallas in the prior fiscal year  

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Under **MSRB Rule G-37** (pay-to-play), the $250 de minimis exception only applies if the MFP is **entitled to vote** for the candidate. Since the MFP lives in Chicago and cannot vote in Dallas, *any* contribution to a Dallas candidate triggers the penalty: the firm is **banned from negotiated municipal underwriting** with that municipality for **2 full years**. **(A)** is wrong because the $250 exception does not apply when the MFP cannot vote for the candidate. **(B)** is wrong because the penalty falls on the firm, not just the individual. **(D)** is fabricated.

### Q19 (Scenario)

A registered representative buys two $200 tickets to a professional basketball game. She gives both tickets to a valued client and does not attend the game herself. How is this treated under FINRA's gift and entertainment rules?

**A.** It is considered business entertainment and is exempt from the $100 gift limit  
**B.** It is considered a $400 gift and violates the $100 per person, per year gift limit  
**C.** It is permitted as a branded promotional item exempt from the gift limit  
**D.** It is permitted because sporting event tickets are categorically exempt from gift rules  

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Under FINRA's rules, event tickets are only classified as **business entertainment** (exempt from the $100 gift limit) if the representative **attends the event with the client**. Since the representative gave the tickets but did not attend, this is treated as a **gift** of $400, which far exceeds the **$100 per person, per year** limit. **(A)** is wrong specifically because the rep did not attend. **(C)** is wrong because tickets are not promotional items. **(D)** is wrong because sporting event tickets are not categorically exempt.

### Q20 (Scenario)

A registered representative volunteers on weekends at a local non-profit animal shelter. She receives no compensation of any kind for this work. Must she report this activity to her firm as an Outside Business Activity (OBA)?

**A.** Yes — all activities outside the firm must be reported, regardless of compensation  
**B.** Yes — but only if the volunteer work exceeds 10 hours per week  
**C.** No — purely volunteer work for a non-profit where no compensation is received is exempt from OBA reporting requirements  
**D.** No — but only if the non-profit is registered as a 501(c)(3) organization  

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** **Passive investments** and **purely volunteer work for a non-profit** (where zero compensation is received) are specifically exempt from OBA reporting requirements. The representative does not need to provide written notice to her firm. **(A)** overstates the rule — not all outside activities require notice. **(B)** is fabricated — there is no hours-per-week threshold. **(D)** is wrong because the exemption is based on the volunteer/no-compensation nature of the work, not the organization's specific tax classification.
